What it is

GlassHood connects to a running system — today a solution on Google Cloud, read from Azure — and builds one live picture of it: every part, every connection between them and their health, refreshed every cycle. It merges what it discovers with the system's declared design, so the gap between the design and what actually runs is visible. Rules and anomaly detection raise alerts from the measurements, and every 3 hours an AI review reads each part's logs and raises its own alarms, labelled as found by the AI review. An AI overview then says in a few sentences how the whole system is doing and what needs attention, and each part has its own. Operators and business readers see the same dashboard, and nothing they do there changes the monitored system.

The problem it solves

Integration pipelines fail quietly. A connection stops, a message no longer arrives, a service is replaced and an interface is left behind, and the first sign is often a wrong result further down the chain. Each team watches its own system with its own tool, so nobody sees the whole chain at once, and the design on paper drifts away from what actually runs. GlassHood puts the whole chain in one view, compares it with the declared design, and points at the part where it breaks.

How it works

Read-only by design

GlassHood installs nothing in the system it watches. Toward the monitored cloud project, every call is list, get or describe; nothing creates, changes or deletes anything there. Read-only is enforced twice: by that call surface, and by the identity itself, a short-lived, viewer-only federated token with no stored keys. Even a full compromise of the engine cannot write to the monitored cloud project, and there is no stored cloud key to steal.

Live topology against declared design

Auto-discovery alone misses nodes it cannot enumerate and carries no intended state; a declarative manifest alone goes stale the moment it is written. GlassHood merges both: live discovery is overlaid with declarative YAML manifests into a single graph, rebuilt with fresh metrics every cycle. Because observed reality and declared intent sit in one model, the gap between what was designed and what is actually running is visible, and change between cycles is tracked.

Deterministic signals first, AI on top

The measurements are checked by a deterministic and statistical tier: threshold rules, anomaly detection over a rolling baseline, and a correlator that ties anomalies to operational alerts. AI runs on top: it writes the short overviews, and every 3 hours it reviews each part's logs and raises its own alarms, kept apart from the others and labelled as found by the AI review. An AI alarm must quote the log lines it rests on, or it is dropped, and a critical one stays until a signed-in person acknowledges it. A signed-in operator can ask the AI to investigate one alarm deeper and get a report; that investigation may search the web for the error.

Compliance as a byproduct of observation

Compliance is not a pre-baked attestation. Reports are generated on demand from what the engine actually observed (rule alerts, CVE findings and anomalies), with a per-subsystem ALCOA+ audit trail: the evidence is the observation. The reports are aligned to EU GMP Annex 11, ALCOA+ and 21 CFR Part 11 and support human review. They are unsigned and are not an independent validation or certification; a qualified person reviews and adopts every artifact.
